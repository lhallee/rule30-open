# Under a center column repeating 00001, column 1 at the first white step has isolated 1s and no run of exactly two 0s

Let the center column repeat the block $00001$ and let it drive the right half, from any initial right half, finite
or not. Each period of the block has four white steps; at the first of them, the one just after the black step, read
column 1. From the 5th reading on, these readings never contain $11$ or $1001$: every 1 is isolated and no run of 0s
between two 1s has length exactly 2. The proof is a rank certificate on a strip of 12 columns, checked by the kernel:
whatever the cells beyond column 12 do, the strip falls within 29 steps into a closed set of states on which no reading
completes one of the two words. It is the certificate of [ThreeRuns](ThreeRuns.md) for a second block.

## Statement

The center is $c_{ph}(t) = 1$ exactly when $(t + ph) \bmod 5 = 4$ (`ctr ph t`, letter $(t + ph) \bmod 5$ of
`w00001`), and $F = \mathrm{drv}(c_{ph}, R)$ is the kit's driven right half: column 0 is $c_{ph}$, column $p \ge 1$
starts as $R(p)$ and follows Rule 30. The first white steps are the $t$ with $(t + ph) \bmod 5 = 0$; the $i$-th of
them is $t_i = 5i + ((5 - ph \bmod 5) \bmod 5)$, and the $i$-th reading is $b_i = F(t_i, 1)$ (`reading R ph i`).
`SparseOnes b i0` says that for every $i \ge i_0$ neither $b_i b_{i+1} = 11$ nor $b_i b_{i+1} b_{i+2} b_{i+3} = 1001$.

1. `runs_00001`. For every $R : \mathbb{N} \to \mathrm{Bool}$ and every $ph$, `SparseOnes (reading R ph) 5`.
2. `runs_00001_evolve`. For every row `init` and time $T$ with `evolve init t 0 = w00001.getD ((t - T) % 5) false`
   for all $t \ge T$, the readings $j \mapsto$ `evolve init (T + 5 j) 1` satisfy `SparseOnes _ 5`.
3. `no_11_time_00001`, `no_1001_time_00001`. The same by times: if $(u + ph) \bmod 5 = 0$, then
   $F(u,1)F(u+5,1)$ is not $11$ when $u \ge 24$, and $F(u,1)F(u+5,1)F(u+10,1)F(u+15,1)$ is not $1001$ when
   $u \ge 14$. In each case the last reading is at time 29 or later.
4. `Runs00001.rank_bound`. Along every driven right half, the certificate's rank of the state at time $t$ is at most
   $29 - t$.

## Argument

1. **A finite state.** Columns $1, \dots, 12$ at time $t + 1$ are a function of columns $1, \dots, 12$ at time $t$,
   the center at time $t$ (known from the phase) and column 13 at time $t$, which is unknown. A state is a phase
   $p \in \{0, \dots, 4\}$, a window $w < 2^{12}$ (bit $i$ is column $i + 1$) and the last three readings $s < 8$
   (newest in bit 0). A step goes to phase $p + 1 \bmod 5$, to the window `stripStep 12 (w00001.getD p false) z w`
   for column 13 equal to $z$, and at phase 0 it appends the reading $w \bmod 2$, column 1 before the step, to $s$.
2. **The closed good set and its ranks.** The good states form the greatest set $G$ that is closed under both
   successors and on which no step at phase 0 completes $11$ or $1001$; $G$ has 139,758 of the
   $5 \cdot 8 \cdot 2^{12} = 163{,}840$ states. Rank $G$ as 0, and give rank $k$ to a state outside the lower ranks
   whose two successors both have rank below $k$. Every state gets a rank, and the largest is 29.
3. **The kernel check** (`nodeOK`, `ok_p`, `parts_ok`). For every state: its rank is at most 29; at rank 0 both
   successors have rank 0 and, at phase 0, the reading does not complete a word (`badB`, which compares
   $(2s + b) \bmod 2^{|u|}$ with each word $u$); at positive rank both successors have smaller rank. The ranks are
   packed 5 bits per window into 40 numbers, one per phase and readings, and each phase is checked by
   `decide +kernel`. The check needs neither the construction of step 2 nor its claim of greatest set; any table that
   passes it proves the theorem.
4. **The driven half follows the states** (`win`, `win_enc`, `win_succ`, `suf`). The window of $F$ at time $t$ is
   encoded in the kit's form `EncF F 12 t 0`; the kit's `encF_step` moves it one step with column 13 as the unknown
   cell, and the center at time $t$ is letter $(t + ph) \bmod 5$ of the block. The readings start as $s = 0$.
5. **Rank falls to 0 by time 29** (`rank_bound`). At time 0 the rank is at most 29; a positive rank drops by at least
   1 per step and rank 0 stays 0.
6. **No word after time 29** (`no_bad`, `suf_period`). From time 29 on the state has rank 0, so a reading at phase 0
   completes no word against the stored readings. Five steps after a phase 0 step the stored readings have shifted by
   the reading of that step (`sNext_ne` at the four other phases), so a word whose last reading is at time 29 or
   later is checked against its true readings (`bad_11`, `bad_1001`).
7. **Indices** (`runs_00001`). The reading $b_{i+k}$ is at time $t_i + 5k$, and $t_i \ge 25$ for $i \ge 5$.
8. **Every row** (`evolve_eq_drv`, `runs_00001_evolve`). From time $T$ on, the cells $p \ge 1$ of a row whose center
   repeats $00001$ from $T$ are the right half driven by $c_0$ from the row at time $T$, by induction on the time as in
   [ThreeRuns](ThreeRuns.md). The first white steps are then the times $T + 5j$.

## Relation to the kit

The kit's strip certificates (`Rule30StripCertificates`, `Rule30StripCertificateTable`) force column 1 to be
eventually periodic at the white phases of a block, and masked Jen then excludes the block; neither they nor the
kit's equivalents state a constraint on words of column 1 that leaves it aperiodic. The theorem here is of the kind of [ThreeRuns](ThreeRuns.md): at this phase column 1 is not eventually
constant in samples, and its readings keep up to 0.597 bits per reading (the entropy of the sequences without $11$ and
$1001$).

At the first white step of $00001$ the next center cell is white, so column $-1$ there equals the reading: for a row
whose center repeats $00001$, column $-1$ at the times $T + 5j$ also has isolated 1s and no run of exactly two 0s from
$j = 5$ on. That corollary is not stated in Lean here.

[Reading constraints](../../findings/columns/reading-constraints.md) finds, at width 14, forced entry for $00001$ into
16 words read at all four white phases. [reading_certificates.py](../../computations/reading_certificates.py) searches
each of the 22 blocks there for the cheapest certificate read at one white phase or at all of them; for $00001$ at the
first white step, $11$ alone is forced at width 4 (160 states, rank at most 5), and with $1001$ the narrowest width is
12.

## Reproduce

```bash
python -m computations.reading_certificates --block 00001 --phases 0 --width 12 --words 11 1001 \
    --check Rule30Open/Columns/Runs00001.lean   # forced within 29 steps, and the tables of the module
python -m computations.reading_certificates --block 00001 --phases 0 --width 12 --words 11 1001 --lean   # tables
python -m computations.reading_certificates --survey   # the cheapest certificate of the 22 blocks, about 20 min
```

`tests/test_reading_certificates.py` checks the window step cell by cell, the good set and the ranks against a naive
greatest fixed point and naive backward layers for three blocks, the ranks of `three_runs` for $001$, a direct run
of random halves of $00001$ from every phase, and that the tables of this module are the computed ranks.
