# Reformulations of Problem 1 that did not close

Each item restates Problem 1 exactly or derives a consequence of an eventual period, and each stops at the same place:
a statement about the chaotic part of the pattern that is as hard as the original. They are listed so that nobody
repeats them without a new idea for the step where they stopped.

- **Strobed form.** Under an eventual period $P$, $P$ steps of the fixed word make each half an autonomous map, and the
  left half is a causal, continuous function $\Psi_w$ of the right half's white-time output. The question becomes
  whether $\Psi_w$ maps some finite right half to a finite left half. $\Psi_w$ of the empty right half looks random for
  every word except the stripe and ether words. *Stops at:* controlling $\Psi_w$ on all finite inputs.
- **Group form.** The left half is $n_t \Phi^t$ with $n_t$ in a locally finite group generated by conjugates of local
  flips. The group orders are $2^3$, $2^{12}$, $2^{45}$ and $2^{174}$ for $t = 1$ to 4, about 0.68 of the full tree
  group's exponent: no small hidden structure. *Stops at:* sections of conjugates bring in every power of $\Phi$, so no
  finite recursion falls out.
- **Window counts (Kopra's trick).** Under an eventual period, equal counts of the windows seen infinitely often at two
  consecutive radii would make a window eventually periodic, against Jen, so the counts grow by at least one per
  radius. *Stops at:* an upper bound on the counts, which is not available.
- **Clocks.** Under an eventual period the left half is readable from column 1 at the white times, so that sequence
  must carry the left side's phase modulo every power of two (the left diagonals' periods are unbounded). The right
  half carries the same clock at its edge. *Stops at:* the two clocks agree, and their delays are not controlled.
- **Rightmost difference.** In two left halves driven by one center, the rightmost difference moves right one cell per
  step and never cancels, so it reaches column $-1$ at a known time, which must be white with column 1 differing. This
  is masked Jen at the first difference. *Stops at:* later differences carry no such rigidity.
- **Stable set.** With $S(u, v) = (\sigma u \oplus (u \lor v), u)$ the sideways map on pairs of time sequences, Problem 1
  says no pair (eventually periodic center, driven column 1) lies in the stable set of the fixed point $(0, 0)$.
  Linearizing at $(0, 0)$ gives the Rule 150 sideways map. *Stops at:* the nonlinear stable set is what matters.
- **Thin image.** Under an eventual period the left half is a 2-adic function $\Lambda$ of the right half, many-to-one
  because of the OR, whose image has dimension about the right channel's entropy, a thin Cantor set. *Stops at:*
  whether that set meets the positive integers.
- **Infinite descent.** The finite rows with a purely periodic center column are closed under $P$ steps of the rule.
  *Stops at:* no width-decreasing operation stays in the set: backward steps in the right-edge frame leave it, and
  truncation or removing the leftmost cell breaks the constraint.
- **Virtual columns.** $V(p)(j) = R_j(p + j)$, built from the right diagonals, is continuous in the 2-adic $p$, gives the
  center column at $p = 0$ and the other columns at the integers, and every pair $(V(p), V(p + 1))$ obeys the sideways
  map, so their closure is an odometer. *Stops at:* no virtual column tested ($p < 2^{16}$, and $\pm 1/3$, $\pm 1/5$,
  $\pm 1/7$, $-1$, $-2/3$) looks eventually periodic, and nothing forces one to.
