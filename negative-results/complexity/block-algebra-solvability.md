# Rule 30's k-block algebra is never a quasigroup and its multiplication monoid is solvable for every block size, with the same simple divisors as the linear Rule 150 and the P-complete Rule 110 on the computed range, so this algebraic invariant does not separate fast from hard prediction

**Question.** A classical line of work asks which cellular automata can be predicted faster than by simulation from
the algebra of their local rule. Moore ([Quasi-linear cellular automata](https://arxiv.org/abs/adap-org/9701001),
*Physica D* 103, 1997; [Predicting non-linear cellular automata quickly by decomposing them into linear
ones](https://arxiv.org/abs/patt-sol/9701008), *Physica D* 111, 1998) writes a rule on a staggered lattice as a binary
operation $a * b$. As the abstracts state (the full texts are not summarized here), when the operation is a quasigroup
or group with enough structure (linear over an abelian group, and many nonlinear and nonabelian cases), $t$ steps can
be predicted in parallel time polylogarithmic in $t$ or serial time near $t$, although the rule is nonlinear (serial
times $t$, $t \log t$, $t \log^2 t$, $t^\alpha$ with $\alpha < 2$ and parallel times $\log t$ to $\log^2 t$). On the
other side, Barrington's theorem (*JCSS* 38, 1989) and Barrington and Thérien (*JACM* 35, 1988) put the word problem
of a finite monoid in ACC$^0$ when every subgroup is solvable and make it NC$^1$-complete when a nonsolvable group
divides it. Where does Rule 30's operation sit on this map, and does its position say anything about Problem 3?

**What was done.** For an elementary rule $f$ and a block size $k \ge 2$, a block is $a \in \{0,1\}^k$ and

$$(a * b)_m = f(w_m, w_{m+1}, w_{m+2}), \qquad w = (a_0, \dots, a_{k-1}, b_0, \dots, b_{k-1}),\ 0 \le m < k,$$

which is one step of the rule on the two blocks, written on the $k$ cells one place to the right of $a_0$. The right
translation by $b$ is $\rho_b(a) = a * b$, the left translation by $a$ is $\lambda_a(b) = a * b$, $G_k$ is the group
generated by the bijective right translations, and $M_k$ is the transformation monoid of $\{0,1\}^k$ generated by all
$\rho_b$ and $\lambda_a$. `computations/block_algebra.py` builds the table, computes $|G_k|$ by the Schreier-Sims
algorithm, enumerates $M_k$ where it is small, lists the group induced on the image of each idempotent (isomorphic to
the maximal subgroup there), and lists the periods of the elements of $M_k$. Once every subgroup is known to be
solvable, a prime $p$ gives a simple divisor $C_p$ of $M_k$ exactly when some element has period divisible by $p$
(an element of period divisible by $p$ generates a group with an element of order $p$; conversely, by Cauchy's
theorem, a subgroup of order divisible by $p$ contains one). The test checks the table against direct simulation, the
group orders against brute-force closure, the translations against the kit's states of automaton group 2369, the rank
bounds of the proof below, the periods against the powers of each element, the monoid sizes, and the solvability test
on $A_5$ and $S_4$.

## Outcome

**Theorem 1 (the units are a 2-group, and they are group 2369).** Let $f$ be left-permutive, $f(l, c, r) = l \oplus
h(c, r)$. For every $k$, each $\rho_b$ is the bijection $a_m \mapsto a_m \oplus h(w_{m+1}, w_{m+2})$, in which
coordinate $m$ is flipped by a function of coordinates above $m$ (of $a$, or of $b$, which is fixed). So $\rho_b$
lies in the group $W_k$ of such triangular maps, the iterated wreath product $C_2 \wr \dots \wr C_2$ of order
$2^{2^k - 1}$ (the automorphisms of the depth-$k$ binary tree read from coordinate $k-1$ down, a Sylow 2-subgroup of
$\mathrm{Sym}(2^k)$). Hence $G_k \le W_k$ is a 2-group: nilpotent, so solvable. For Rule 30, $h(c, r) = c \lor r$,
so coordinates $m \le k - 3$ of $\rho_b(a)$ do not read $b$, coordinate $k - 2$ reads $b_0$, and coordinate $k - 1$
reads $b_0 \lor b_1$. So $\rho_b$ depends on $b$ only through $(b_0, b_0 \lor b_1)$, which takes three values, and
for every $k \ge 2$ there are exactly three right translations. Under the bit reversal $y(e) = a_{k-1-e}$ they are the
kit's three states `PhiS 0`, `PhiS 1`, `PhiS 3` modulo $2^k$ (`Rule30Equivalents.AutomatonGroup`, where
`PhiS s x = x ^^^ (2x ||| 4x ||| s)`), with $s = 2 b_0 + (b_0 \lor b_1)$, that is, $s = 0, 1, 3$ for
$(b_0, b_1) = (0, 0), (0, 1), (1, \cdot)$. So **$G_k$ is automaton group 2369 acting on level $k$ of the binary
tree**: it is not a new object, and its 2-group property is the kit's observation that the group is a tree
automorphism group. The test checks the identification for $k = 2, \dots, 5$.

| $k$ | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $\log_2 \lvert G_k \rvert$, Rule 30 (computed) | 3 | 7 | 13 | 25 | 47 |
| $\log_2 \lvert W_k \rvert = 2^k - 1$ | 3 | 7 | 15 | 31 | 63 |

$G_k = W_k$ for $k \le 3$ (computed), and $G_k \ne W_k$ for every $k \ge 4$ (proved): $W_k$ maps onto $C_2^k$ (the
sign of each level), so it needs $k$ generators, and $G_k$ has three. Rule 45 gives the same orders; Rules 90 and 150
give $2^2, 2^4, 2^5, 2^7, 2^8$ and $2^3, 2^5, 2^6, 2^8, 2^9$ for $k = 2..6$ (the abelian translation groups of a
linear rule are small).

**Theorem 2 (the left translations have rank at most 3).** For every elementary rule and $k \ge 2$, coordinates
$m \le k - 3$ of $a * b$ do not read $b$, and coordinates $k-2$, $k-1$ read it only through $(b_0, b_1)$, so
$\lvert \lambda_a(\{0,1\}^k) \rvert \le 4$. For Rule 30, if $a_{k-1} = 1$ the top two coordinates are
$(a_{k-2} \oplus 1, 1 \oplus (b_0 \lor b_1))$, two values; if $a_{k-1} = 0$ they are $(a_{k-2} \oplus b_0, b_0 \lor b_1)$,
which takes the three values from $(b_0, b_1) = 00, 01, 1\cdot$. So $\lambda_a$ has rank at most 3, for every $k$.
In particular **the $k$-block algebra of Rule 30 defined above is not a quasigroup for any $k$** ($3 < 2^k$), so it is
not isotopic to any group, and Moore's quasigroup and group theorems do not apply to these encodings. The statement
concerns this block encoding only: other recodings of the rule as a binary operation (other alignments of the blocks,
other alphabets) are not covered. The same holds for every elementary rule once $k \ge 3$; at $k = 2$ the
bipermutive Rules 90 and 150 are quasigroups (their $\lambda_a$ are bijections) and Rule 30 is not.

**Theorem 3 (the monoid is solvable for every $k$).** For Rule 30 and every $k \ge 2$, every subgroup of $M_k$ is a
2-group or embeds in $S_3$; so every simple group dividing $M_k$ is $C_2$ or $C_3$, and $M_k$ is solvable.

*Proof.* An element of $M_k$ is a word in the generators. If the word contains some $\lambda_a$, its image has at most
3 points, since $\lvert \mathrm{im}(s \circ t) \rvert \le \min(\lvert \mathrm{im}\, s \rvert, \lvert \mathrm{im}\, t
\rvert)$ (Theorem 2). Otherwise it lies in $G_k$, a 2-group (Theorem 1). A subgroup of a transformation monoid lies in
one group $\mathcal H$-class, with identity an idempotent $e$, and acts faithfully on $\mathrm{im}\, e$ (if $s, s'$ in
it agree on $\mathrm{im}\, e$ then $s = se = s'e = s'$), so it embeds in $\mathrm{Sym}(\mathrm{im}\, e)$. If
$e = \mathrm{id}$ the subgroup lies in the units, which are $G_k$ because a word containing a map of rank below $2^k$ is
not a bijection; otherwise $e$ has rank below $2^k$, so it is a word containing some $\lambda_a$, and
$\lvert \mathrm{im}\, e \rvert \le 3$. By the Krohn-Rhodes theory every simple group dividing a finite monoid divides
one of its subgroups, and the simple divisors of 2-groups and of subgroups of $S_3$ are $C_2$ and $C_3$. $\square$

$C_2$ occurs for every $k$ (in $G_k$). $C_3$ occurs for $k = 2, 3, 4$ (computed): $M_2$ and $M_3$ have 180 and 7,432
elements with groups $1$, $C_2$, $S_3$ at the idempotents of rank 1, 2, 3, and $M_4$ has 856,080 elements, among them
elements of period 3. For $k \ge 5$ only the bound of Theorem 3 is claimed.

The same proof gives, for every left-permutive elementary rule and every $k$, subgroups that are 2-groups or embed in
$S_4$ (at $k = 2$ every subgroup lies in $S_4$ anyway), so solvable monoids with simple divisors among $C_2$ and
$C_3$. This includes the linear Rules 90 and 150, but their divisors differ (computed, element periods): for
$k = 2, \dots, 5$ the periods in Rule 90's monoid are 1, 2, 4 only, so its only simple divisor there is $C_2$ ($M_3$
has 160 elements and groups of order 1, 2, 8 at its idempotents), while Rule 150's monoid has elements of period 3 for
$k = 2, \dots, 5$ ($M_2 = S_4$; $M_3$ has 712 elements and a group of order 12 at its rank-4 idempotents), so its
simple divisors are $C_2$ and $C_3$, as for Rule 30.

**Rule 110 has the same simple divisors on the computed range ($k \le 5$).** No $\rho_b$ of Rule 110 is a bijection:
for $k \ge 3$ coordinate 0 of $\rho_b(a)$ is the only coordinate that reads $a_0$, and every value of $(a_1, a_2)$
occurs, so a bijective $\rho_b$ would need $f(l, a_1, a_2)$ to be injective in $l$ for every $(a_1, a_2)$, that is,
Rule 110 left-permutive, which it is not ($f(0, 0, 0) = f(1, 0, 0) = 0$); for $k = 2$ this is computed. For $k \ge 3$
no $\lambda_a$ is a bijection either (Theorem 2), so the units of $M_k$ are trivial. At $k = 2$ one left translation is
a bijection and the units form a group of order 4; every subgroup lies in $\mathrm{Sym}(4)$. The monoids
$M_2, \dots, M_5$ have 236, 4,580, 50,393 and 645,120 elements. At $k = 2, 3$ the groups at the non-identity
idempotents are $1, C_2, S_3$ and, at $k = 3$, $S_4$, all solvable. At $k = 4, 5$ the monoid has no non-identity
idempotent of rank 5 or more, so every subgroup embeds in $S_4$ and is solvable, and the element periods are 1, 2, 3,
4, so $C_2$ and $C_3$ both divide $M_4$ and $M_5$. Predicting Rule 110 from an arbitrary row is P-complete (Neary and
Woods, ICALP 2006, in [the literature](../../literature/complexity.md)), yet for $k \le 5$ its block monoid is solvable
with the same simple divisors, $C_2$ and $C_3$, as Rule 30's (for Rule 30 these are computed for $k \le 4$).

## Where it stops

1. **Moore's quasigroup and group theorems need the operation to be a quasigroup, and Rule 30's $k$-block operation in
   this encoding is not one for any $k$** (Theorem 2). This does not exclude other recodings, or algebraic prediction
   methods that do not assume a quasigroup. What Rule 30 has instead is one-sided: bijective right translations
   forming a 2-group, which is the left-permutivity that every entry on the right-edge frame already uses, here under
   the name group 2369.
2. **Solvability of the monoid does not reach the triangle.** The value of a cell after $t$ steps is a product
   tree, a term in the free magma whose $2t + 1$ leaves are the input cells, not a word in $M_k$. A word in $M_k$
   describes only a computation in which one block moves and every other argument is a known constant, for example the
   black-wall formula $c(t) = $ bit $t - 1$ of $a^t(0)$ in the kit. Barrington-Thérien bounds apply to words over a
   fixed monoid, and that formula needs level $k \approx t$, where $\lvert G_k \rvert$ is $2^{47}$ already at $k = 6$:
   no fixed $k$ suffices.
3. **The invariant does not separate fast from hard prediction.** Solvability holds for every left-permutive rule at
   every $k$ (proved), for Rule 30 at every $k$ (Theorem 3), and for Rule 110 at $k \le 5$ (computed). The finer
   invariant, the set of simple divisors, does separate Rule 90 ($C_2$ only, $k \le 5$) from Rules 30, 110 and 150
   ($C_2$ and $C_3$), but not along the line of prediction cost: Rule 30 shares its divisors with the linear Rule 150
   (predictable in logarithmic parallel time) and with Rule 110 (P-complete from arbitrary rows), on the computed
   range. The barrier's conclusion, which is a reasoned consequence of items 2 and 3 and not a theorem, is that no
   upper or lower bound for Rule 30's prediction can come from the simple divisors of its block monoid alone. The
   nonsolvable side, which would make word problems NC$^1$-complete, is not available to Rule 30 at any block size
   (Theorem 3).
4. Even a P-completeness theorem from arbitrary rows would not bound $c(n)$ from one seed
   ([the Problem 3 barrier](problem-three-barrier.md)); this route does not get that far.

**Relation to other entries.** The group $G_k$ is the level-$k$ image of the automaton group studied in
[the automaton-group sections](automaton-group-sections.md), which counts states of the transducers of powers
$\Phi^{2^k}$; the orders of the level images and the monoid with the left translations are not there. Theorem 2 is
radius-1 locality: information from the right enters a block through two cells, and for Rule 30 only through the
three values of $(b_0, b_0 \lor b_1)$.

**What would change this.** An algebraic invariant of the block algebra, finer than the simple divisors of its monoid,
that differs between the linear Rule 150 and Rule 110 and is computable for Rule 30; or a decomposition of the
triangle of products into words over a fixed monoid, which Moore obtains for quasigroups and which the rank bound of
Theorem 2 does not provide.
