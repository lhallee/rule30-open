# Rule 30's light-like map is invertible and not exact, has entropy at most 0.68 bits, and its correlations fall below 10^-4 by lag 48 in every test, while Rule 180's has the same shape and is not ergodic

$G = \sigma F$, $(Gy)_i = y_i \oplus (y_{i+1} \lor y_{i+2})$, is Rule 30 in the frame moving right at the speed of
light; it acts on the half-lines $X_0 = \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$ and preserves the uniform measure $\lambda$. Whether
$(X_0, G, \lambda)$ is ergodic is the open item 6 of [light-like rigidity](lightlike-rigidity.md), whose positive
branch needs it. This entry settles the part about exactness, bounds the entropy, gives the correlations exactly to
lag 29 and by sampling to lag 1,024, and shows with a sibling rule that the structure the question is usually posed
in does not decide it.

## Results

1. **$G$ is invertible almost everywhere, so it is not exact** (paper; the combinatorial core in Lean,
   [LightLine](../../Rule30Open/RandomRows/LightLine.md)). Two half-lines with the same image agree left of every $010$
   of the image, and $\lambda$-almost every image shows $010$ arbitrarily far right. So $G$ is a measure-preserving
   automorphism modulo $\lambda$, $\bigcap_n G^{-n}\mathcal{B} = \mathcal{B}$, and $(X_0, G, \lambda)$ is its own natural
   extension. Of the properties Q2 lists, exactness fails; ergodicity, weak mixing, mixing, the K property and
   Bernoullicity remain open, and for each of them the half-line system and $\sigma F$ on two-sided rows agree (the
   two-sided system is generated by the increasing factors on $[-n, \infty)$, each a copy of the half-line system).
2. **The ergodic questions are questions about one twisted operator** (paper). $L^2(X_0)$ is the constants plus the
   $G$-invariant spaces $V_s$ spanned by the Walsh characters with least cell $s$, and on each $V_s$ the Koopman
   operator is a copy of the unitary $T g = (-1)^{y_0 \lor y_1}\, g \circ G$ on $L^2(X_0)$. So $G$ is ergodic, weakly
   mixing or mixing exactly when $T$ has no fixed vector, no eigenvalue, or $\langle T^k f, g \rangle \to 0$ for all
   $f, g$. Writing $h = h_0(y_{\ge 1}) + (-1)^{y_0} h_1(y_{\ge 1})$ turns $Th = h$ into
   $(h_0, h_1)\circ G = M (h_0, h_1)$ on $X_1$ with a cocycle $M$ in the dihedral group of order 8: the swap if
   $y_1 = y_2 = 0$, the quarter turn $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ if
   $(y_1, y_2) = (0, 1)$, and $\mathrm{diag}(-1, 1)$ if $y_1 = 1$. On the block $y_{[0,m]}$, $G$ acts by the
   frozen-boundary steps of the kit's automaton group 2369 (`AutomatonGroup.PhiS`), driven by the letters
   $(c^{m+1}_t \lor c^{m+2}_t,\, c^{m+1}_t)$ of the next two light lines: mixing holds exactly when, for every $m$, the
   random product of these steps, driven by that stationary letter process, equidistributes every block. There is no
   coset obstruction: every step lies in the coset $\Phi N$ of the normal closure $N$ of $\langle \sigma, \tau\rangle$,
   but $N$ acts transitively on every level, because $\Phi$ fixes $0$ and the group acts transitively (computed to level
   22, and for every level from the group's self-replication, which the kit cites from the classification of Bondarenko
   et al.).
3. **Entropy at most 0.6793 bits per step** (computed, exact). $h_\lambda(G)$ equals the entropy of the letter process,
   since the blocks are a skew product over it with finite fiber, so
   $h_\lambda(G) \le H(s_{k-1} \mid s_0, \dots, s_{k-2})$ for every $k$. The exact law of $s_0, \dots, s_{28}$
   (counts out of $4^{29}$, 62,856,511 words of positive probability) gives
   $H_{29} - H_{28} = 0.679237$ bits, below $\log 2$. The conditional entropies decrease slowly (0.7102 at $k = 20$,
   0.6875 at 26, 0.6792 at 29); no lower bound above 0 is proved.
4. **The light-line autocorrelation $\rho(k) = \mathbb{E}(-1)^{(G^k y)_0 \oplus y_0}$** (computed). Exactly, as
   integers over $4^k$, for $k \le 29$ (table below), and from $2.5 \cdot 10^{11}$ samples (240 rows of $2^{20}$
   light lines over 1,100 steps, standard error $2.6 \cdot 10^{-6}$) for $k \le 1{,}024$: $|\rho(k)|$ falls from
   $1.6 \cdot 10^{-3}$ at $k = 26$ to $1.6 \cdot 10^{-4}$ at $k = 32$ to $4 \cdot 10^{-5}$ at $k = 36$, and is at most
   $8.7 \cdot 10^{-6}$ for every sampled $k$ from 40 to 1,024 (the largest $|z|$ is 2.5, at $k = 512$, among the 26
   lags sampled there).
5. **Every twisted correlation tested decays** (computed). For all 64 pairs of characters $\chi_S, \chi_R$ with
   $S, R \subseteq \{0,1,2,3\}$ and $\min S = \min R = 0$ (when $\min S \ne \min R$ the correlation is exactly 0:
   flipping the cell $\min(S \cup R)$ changes one side only), $C(S, R, k) = \mathbb{E}\,\chi_S(G^k y)\chi_R(y)$ has largest modulus $0.069$ at $k = 10$,
   $5.7 \cdot 10^{-3}$ at $k = 20$, $6.5 \cdot 10^{-4}$ at $k = 30$, $1.6 \cdot 10^{-4}$ at $k = 40$, and at most
   $8.7 \cdot 10^{-5}$ over the 832 values with $48 \le k \le 512$, of which 3 have $|z| > 3$ (2.2 expected by chance;
   48 rows of $2^{18}$ lines over 600 steps, standard error about $1.6 \cdot 10^{-5}$).
6. **The cocycles that weak mixing reduces to** (paper and computed). If $T h = \pm h$ has a solution with
   $|h| = 1$, then $r(y) = h(y)\,h(\tau_0 y)$, $\tau_0$ the flip of cell 0 (which commutes with $G$), is a $\pm 1$
   function of $y_{\ge 1}$ with $r \circ G = (-1)^{1 + y_1} r$, and $e(y) = r(y)\, r(\tau_1 y)$ is a $\pm 1$ function of
   $y_{\ge 2}$ with $e \circ G = -e$, so $G^2$ is not ergodic. So in the case where a cell is a function of the cells
   to its right everywhere, non-ergodicity forces the eigenvalue $-1$, which is the twisted equation for the hold
   cocycle $[y_0 = y_1 = 0]$, with correlation $(-1)^k \rho(k)$; and the cocycle $1 + y_0$ (the parity of the white
   cells of a light line) is a coboundary. Its correlation $\rho_\pi(k)$ is exactly $0$ for odd $k$ (flip cell 0) and
   decays for even $k$: $-0.5, 0.375, -0.203, \dots, 4.35 \cdot 10^{-3}$ at $k = 2, 4, 6, \dots, 28$.
7. **The shape of the question does not decide it** (Lean core and paper,
   [LightLike180](../../Rule30Open/Analogs/LightLike180.md)). Rule 180's light-like map
   $y_i \oplus (y_{i+1} \land \lnot y_{i+2})$ has everything item 2 uses (permutive in its own cell, nonlinear in the two to
   its right, a skew product over a copy of itself, the product measure with trivial tail) and is not ergodic: its
   light line 0 is constant on an invariant set of measure $2/3$, and its $\rho(k) = 1 - 2^{-2^{v_2(k)}}$ for $k \le 22$.
   The other two classes of left-permutive nonlinear elementary rules decay: by sampling, Rule 120's light-like
   $\rho$ is $0.141, 0.048, 0.0076, 0.0004$ at lags 32, 64, 128, 256 and Rule 45's is $-0.009, -0.002$ at 32, 64
   (6 rows of 4,096 lines over 1,024 steps). Rule 30's is the fastest.

## Method

**The letter law.** Light line $j - 1$ is $y_{j-1}$ plus the running parity of $c^j \lor c^{j+1}$, so the letters of
level $j - 1$ come from those of level $j$ through a two-state transducer started in a fair state, and the law $\mu_K$
of the letters at times $0, \dots, K - 1$ is the same at every level. A letter of level $j$ at time $t$ depends on
$y_{[j, j+2t+1]}$; a letter appended at time $K - 1$ to $\mu_{K-1}$ is outside the light cone after two transducer
steps, so $\mu_K$ is two steps of the transducer applied to $\mu_{K-1}$ with any letter appended. The C program keeps
$\mu_K$ as integer counts out of $4^K$ on its support (sorted 64-bit words), which is exact to $K = 31$; it agrees with
brute force over all $2^{2k+1}$ inputs to $k = 6$, with the Python version to $K = 10$, and with the sampled values.

**Sampling.** Rows of $2^{20}$ (or $2^{18}$) cells plus a margin are evolved as 64-bit words; the first cells of each
row at each time are the light lines, and each correlation is averaged over lines and starting times, with the standard
error taken over independent rows.

**Rule 180.** [LightLike180](../../Rule30Open/Analogs/LightLike180.md) proves in Lean that each light line is constant
on the white times of the next one; with the flip of cell 1 this gives $\lambda(\text{line 0 constant to } T) =
2/3 + 4^{-T}/3$, which brute force over all inputs confirms for $T \le 12$.

## Where it stops

- **Ergodicity, mixing, K, Bernoulli** stay open. The reductions of item 2 are exact but self-similar: the base of each
  skew product is a copy of the whole system, so every route that assumes a property of the base assumes the
  conclusion. Item 7 shows that a proof has to use the OR itself.
- **Positive entropy** is not proved. The cells with distinct feet give $h(\sigma^a G^b) \ge |a| \log 2$ and nothing for
  $a = 0$.
- **Exact correlations** cost two sorts of the support per lag; the support grows by a factor of about 1.68 per lag
  (37,331,181 words at $K = 28$, 62,856,511 at $K = 29$), $K = 29$ ran in 42 s within a 10.5 GB memory limit, and the
  64-bit words cap the program at $K = 31$.
- The routes tried and where they stop are in [the soft routes](../../negative-results/random-rows/lightlike-soft-routes.md).

## Reproduce

```bash
python -m computations.light_line --exact 29                         # the table and the entropy; 45 s, under 10 GB
python -m computations.light_line --mc 20 1100 240 --masks 1         # rho(k) to k = 1,024; about 3 min, 4 cores
python -m computations.light_line --mc 18 600 48 --masks 1,3,5,7,9,11,13,15   # item 5; about 70 s
python -m computations.light_line --siblings 22 --rule180 12 --forbidden 10 --transitive 22   # about 1 min
```

## Relation to the kit and the literature

The kit proves that $F$ is mixing of all orders and invertible almost everywhere (`Mixing`), that every line of
velocity other than 1 is a sequence of fair coins, and excepts velocity 1 (`Mixing.measurePreserving_line`,
`Mixing.measure_diag_eq`). Shereshevsky (1992) and Kleveland (1997) prove mixing and the K property for automata
permutive in an extreme cell at a nonzero offset; $G$ is permutive only at offset 0, and Rule 180 shows the offset-0
case can fail. The conjecture of Shirvani and Rogers cited in [light-like rigidity](lightlike-rigidity.md) (record seen
only through search results) would make $G$ ergodic; Rule 180's light-like map, onto and neither the identity nor the
inversion, is not ergodic, so the conjecture fails if it is stated for all onto automata; its text was not read here.
